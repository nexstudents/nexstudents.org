Pre-Algebra 2.7 Solving Equations

Decimal Operations and Equations with Decimals

Decimals can be negative too, and Sam uses the same sign rules he already knows to work with them.

Teacher Notes

Not started yet.

Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson your student should be able to add, subtract, multiply and divide positive and negative decimals with the integer sign rules, check an answer by estimating, and solve one-step equations with decimals.

Where Students Get Stuck

Losing the negative sign. The sign comes first: decide it from the sign rules, then do the digits.

Subtracting a negative. Rewrite it as adding the opposite, so -14.62 - (-30.805) is -14.62 + 30.805.

A misplaced decimal point in the answer. An estimate with friendly numbers (about -40 divided by -2) catches an answer that is ten times too big or too small.

Book Pages

McDougal Littell Pre-Algebra (2008), pages 102 to 107. Read free on archive.org: https://archive.org/details/prealgebra0000unse_z0p2/page/102/mode/1up

Suggested Videos

Optional. Watch one first, then press Add to put it in the Visual Panel for a student.

  • How to Solve Equations | One-Step & Two-Step | Math with Mr. JMath with Mr. JNo decimal-specific video was found, but this reviews one-step equations of every kind, and the same moves work with decimals.

Key Vocabulary

Absolute value
How far a number is from zero. It is never negative, so the absolute value of -2.9 is 2.9.
Estimate
A close answer you get with friendly numbers, used to check that an exact answer makes sense.
Opposite
The number the same distance from zero on the other side, like 5 and -5. Subtracting a number is adding its opposite.
