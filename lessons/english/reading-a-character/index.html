English 7 U1-L8 Out Here on My Own

Reading a Character

Writers rarely tell you what a character is like. They show you a clue and let you work it out.

Teacher Notes

Not started yet.

Lesson Goal

Students will learn what an inference is (a clue from the text plus what the reader already knows), practice it on the characters in O. Henry's The Gift of the Magi, and answer questions that give a line and ask what it shows about Della or Jim.

Key Concepts

A character is a person or animal who takes part in the action of a story. An inference is an educated guess based on evidence: a clue the text gives plus something the reader already knows. The text says what Della does and says; the reader supplies what that kind of action usually means.

An inference isn't a fact stated in the text, and it isn't a wild guess either. It has to be backed by a line.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students retell the line instead of saying what it shows, or they guess something the text doesn't support. Ask for the clue and for what he knew that connected it to his guess. Another slip is calling a plot fact an inference: the narrator says Della sold her hair, which is a fact, and that she loves Jim is the inference.

Teaching Suggestion

At each stop have the student say his inference aloud before the page gives its own, and then ask him to name the clue and the thing he knew. The same two-part check works on every question.

Key Vocabulary

Character
A person or animal who takes part in the action of a story.
Inference
An educated guess based on evidence: a clue from the text plus what you already know.
Evidence
The clue in the story that backs up your guess, such as a line a character says or does.
Magi
The wise men who brought gifts to the baby Jesus.
