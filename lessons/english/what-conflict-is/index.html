English 7 U1-L1 Out Here on My Own

What Conflict Is

Somebody wants something and somebody else wants the opposite. Find the two sides before the fight starts.

Teacher Notes

Not started yet.

Lesson Goal

Students will learn what a conflict is, name the two sides of the main conflicts in the first half of Kipling's Rikki-Tikki-Tavi, and practice writing down reactions, questions, and guesses while they read.

Key Concepts

A conflict is a struggle between opposing sides. The sides can be two characters who want opposite things, or a character and a force, such as a flood. The opponent is whoever or whatever stands in the way of the character we follow. In this half of the story the clashes are Rikki-tikki against Nag and Nagaina, Rikki-tikki against Karait, and, before any of them, Rikki-tikki against a summer flood.

Where Students Get Stuck

Students often name the setting or the whole plot instead of the sides. If a student says the conflict is the garden, ask who wants what in the garden, and who wants the opposite. Another common slip is putting the wrong character on a side: Darzee and his wife are on Rikki-tikki's side, since Nag ate their baby.

Teaching Suggestion

Keep a notes sheet beside the student while the story plays. Three notes are plenty: one reaction, one question, one guess. Afterward, ask the student to read the notes aloud and sort the names into two sides before answering any question.

Key Vocabulary

Conflict
A struggle between two opposing sides: two characters who want opposite things, or a character and a force like a flood.
Opponent
The character or force on the other side of a conflict, the one standing in the way of the character you're following.
Force
Something with no face, like a flood or a blizzard, that works against a character the way an enemy would.
Dialogue with the Text
Talking back to a story as you read it, by writing down reactions, questions, and guesses on a sheet of paper.
