How the placing works

1
Start at the level just finished

Not the level you hope for. Level B is grade 6.

2
The score picks the next move

85% or above, climb a level. Under 60%, drop one. In between, that is the placement.

3
Read the skill rows

Main idea, inference, vocabulary in context, supporting detail, text structure, author's purpose — each scored separately. Missing all three inference questions means something very different from three scattered misses.

4
Teach the gap, not the grade

Supporting detail and vocabulary come back quickly. Inference and text structure are the slow ones, and the ones that matter most later.

What this does not do

No percentile, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

Saying "your child reads at a 6.4 level" requires a tested reference population of thousands of students. We do not have one. That norming is the actual product the paid tests are selling, and inventing a number without it would be dishonest.

What this gives you instead: where your student holds up and where they stop, measured against a published skill sequence and against their own earlier results. For deciding what to teach on Monday, that is the more useful of the two.

If you need a normed score on paper for your state, a proctored standardized test is still the right tool. This is not trying to replace that.
