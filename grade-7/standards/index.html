What Missouri Expects.

All 160 Missouri Learning Standards for 7th grade, across English, History, Math and Science, lined up against the year plan so you can see the holes. Every subject links to the state document it came from.

160Standards checked
65Covered
56Partial
39Not covered

the year plan teaches it met, but not by name, or met in another subject nothing in the year plan touches it

This is a gap-spotting tool, not a syllabus. Missouri homeschool law is hours based, 1,000 a year with 600 in the core subjects, and there is no state test. DESE says it plainly too: the standards do not dictate curriculum. So a “not covered” row is a place this year and the public 7th grade differ. It is a difference, not a debt.

Every standard below is quoted from Missouri’s own 2016 board-approved documents, and every subject links out to the document it came from. The coverage column is a judgement made by hand against the year plan, once, in September 2026.

M Mathematics 50 standards

Grade 7 math is written per grade. Five domains, 50 standards. Taught from Glencoe Mathematics: Applications and Connections, Course 2.

25 covered · 21 partial · 4 not covered

Source: MLS Math Expanded Expectations, Grade 7 · Missouri DESE

Ratios and Proportional Relationships

  • Analyze proportional relationships and use them to solve problems
  • 7.RP.A.1Compute unit rates, including those that involve complex fractions, with like or different units.PartialU11-1, U11-2Rates are taught; complex-fraction unit rates are not.
  • 7.RP.A.2aDetermine when two quantities are in a proportional relationship.CoveredU11-3, U11-4
  • 7.RP.A.2bIdentify and/or compute the constant of proportionality (unit rate).PartialU11-2Unit rate is taught, the phrase constant of proportionality is not.
  • 7.RP.A.2cExplain what a point (x, y) on the graph of a proportional relationship means in terms of the situation.PartialU14-4Graphing two-variable equations, not read back to a situation.
  • 7.RP.A.2dRecognize that the graph of any proportional relationship will pass through the origin.Not coveredNothing in Course 2 says a proportional graph passes through the origin.
  • 7.RP.A.3Solve problems involving ratios, rates, percentages and proportional relationships.CoveredU12-1 through U12-9

Number and Sense Operations

  • Apply and extend previous understandings of operations to add, subtract, multiply and divide rational numbers.
  • 7.NS.A.1aAdd and subtract rational numbers.CoveredU2-1, U5-3, U5-4, U7-4, U7-5
  • 7.NS.A.1bRepresent addition and subtraction on a horizontal or vertical number line.PartialU7-1, U7-2Number line is used for ordering, not for modelling the operation.
  • 7.NS.A.1cDescribe situations and show that a number and its opposite have a sum of 0 (additive inverses).CoveredU7-1, U7-5
  • 7.NS.A.1dUnderstand subtraction of rational numbers as adding the additive inverse.CoveredU7-5
  • 7.NS.A.1eDetermine the distance between two rational numbers on the number line is the absolute value of their difference.PartialU7-1Absolute value is introduced; distance as a difference is not.
  • 7.NS.A.1fInterpret sums and differences of rational numbers.PartialU7-4, U7-5
  • 7.NS.A.2aMultiply and divide rational numbers.CoveredU2-4, U2-7, U5-5, U5-10, U7-7, U7-8
  • 7.NS.A.2bDetermine that a number and its reciprocal have a product of 1 (multiplicative inverse).CoveredU5-10
  • 7.NS.A.2cUnderstand that every quotient of integers (with non-zero divisor) is a rational number.PartialU7-8
  • 7.NS.A.2dConvert a rational number to a decimal.CoveredU4-7
  • 7.NS.A.2eUnderstand that all rational numbers can be written as fractions or decimal numbers that terminate or repeat.CoveredU4-7
  • 7.NS.A.2fInterpret products and quotients of rational numbers by describing real-world contexts.PartialU5-5, U7-7
  • 7.NS.A.3Solve problems involving the four arithmetic operations with rational numbers.CoveredU2, U5, U7

Expressions, Equations and Inequalities

  • Use properties of operations to generate equivalent expressions.
  • 7.EEI.A.1Apply properties of operations to simplify and to factor linear algebraic expressions with rational coefficients.PartialU1-8, U5-9Properties are taught. Factoring a linear expression is not.
  • 7.EEI.A.2Understand how to use equivalent expressions to clarify quantities in a problem.PartialU12-8
  • Solve problems using numerical and algebraic expressions and equations.
  • 7.EEI.B.3aConvert between equivalent forms of the same number.CoveredU4-7, U11-8, U11-9
  • 7.EEI.B.3bAssess the reasonableness of answers using mental computation and estimation strategies.CoveredU1-2, U1-4, U2-3, U2-10, U5-2, U12-3
  • 7.EEI.B.4aWrite and/or solve equations of the form x+p = q and px = q in which p and q are rational numbers.CoveredU6-2, U6-3
  • 7.EEI.B.4bWrite and/or solve two-step equations of the form px + q = r and p(x + q) = r, where p, q and r are rational numbers, and interpret the meaning of the solution in the context of the problem.CoveredU14-2
  • 7.EEI.B.4cWrite, solve and/or graph inequalities of the form px + q > r or px + q < r, where p, q and r are rational numbers.Not coveredInequalities are never taught in Course 2. This is the largest single math gap.

Geometry and Measurement

  • Draw and describe geometrical figures and describe the relationships between them.
  • 7.GM.A.1Solve problems involving scale drawings of real objects and geometric figures, including computing actual lengths and areas from a scale drawing and reproducing the drawing at a different scale.CoveredU11-4, U11-5
  • 7.GM.A.2aDetermine if provided constraints will create a unique triangle through construction.PartialU8-3, U8-4Congruence is taught; constructing to test uniqueness is not.
  • 7.GM.A.2bConstruct special quadrilaterals given specific parameters.PartialU8-3Named and classified, not constructed to given parameters.
  • 7.GM.A.3Describe two-dimensional cross sections of pyramids, prisms, cones and cylinders.PartialU10-1Drawing 3-D figures, not slicing them.
  • 7.GM.A.4aAnalyze the relationships among the circumference, the radius, the diameter, the area and Pi in a circle.CoveredU5-7, U9-8
  • 7.GM.A.4bKnow and apply the formulas for circumference and area of circles to solve problems.CoveredU5-7, U9-8
  • Apply and extend previous understanding of angle measure, area and volume.
  • 7.GM.B.5Use angle properties to write and solve equations for an unknown angle.PartialU8-1Angle pairs are taught; writing an equation for the unknown angle is not.
  • 7.GM.B.6aFind the area of triangles, quadrilaterals and other polygons composed of triangles and rectangles.CoveredU6-7, U9-6, U9-7
  • 7.GM.B.6bFind the volume and surface area of prisms, pyramids and cylinders.PartialU10-3, U10-4, U10-5, U10-6Prisms and cylinders yes, pyramids no.

Data Analysis, Statistics and Probability

  • Use random sampling to draw inferences about a population
  • 7.DSP.A.1aUnderstand that a sample is a subset of a population.PartialU3-7Prediction from data, without the sample and population vocabulary.
  • 7.DSP.A.1bUnderstand that generalizations from a sample are valid only if the sample is representative of the population.PartialU3-8
  • 7.DSP.A.1cUnderstand that random sampling is used to produce representative samples and support valid inferences.Not coveredRandom sampling is never taught.
  • 7.DSP.A.2Use data from multiple samples to draw inferences about a population and investigate variability in estimates of the characteristic of interest.Not coveredNo work with multiple samples or variability between them.
  • Draw informal comparative inferences about two populations.
  • 7.DSP.B.3Analyze different data distributions using statistical measures.CoveredU3-4, U3-5, U3-6
  • 7.DSP.B.4Compare the numerical measures of center, measures of frequency and measures of variability from two random samples to draw inferences about the population.PartialU3-3, U3-5Mean, median, mode and range yes. Interquartile range and mean absolute deviation, no.
  • Develop, use and evaluate probability models.
  • 7.DSP.C.5aDetermine probabilities of simple events.CoveredU4-8, U13-3
  • 7.DSP.C.5bUnderstand that the probability of a chance event is a number between 0 and 1 that expresses the likelihood of the event occurring.CoveredU4-8
  • 7.DSP.C.6aPredict outcomes using theoretical probability.CoveredU13-3
  • 7.DSP.C.6bPerform experiments that model theoretical probability.CoveredU13-3, U13-4
  • 7.DSP.C.6cCompare theoretical and experimental probabilities.CoveredU13-3
  • 7.DSP.C.7aDevelop a uniform probability model by assigning equal probability to all outcomes, and use the model to determine probabilities of events.PartialU13-3
  • 7.DSP.C.7bDevelop a probability model (which may not be uniform) by observing frequencies in data generated from a chance process.PartialU13-5
  • 7.DSP.C.8aRepresent the sample space of a compound event.CoveredU13-1, U13-2, U13-6
  • 7.DSP.C.8bDesign and use a simulation to generate frequencies for compound events.PartialU13-4

The two social studies courses that are not this year

Missouri writes middle school social studies per course, not per grade. American History has 86 standards and is the 8th grade course. Geography has 36 and is its own course, taken in neither of these two years.
