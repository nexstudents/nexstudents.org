Being built.

This is a placement test that watches how a student works a problem, not just whether the final number is right. Getting 28 × 7 wrong because you dropped a carry is a different student from one who cannot multiply, and they need different teaching.

The working has to come first, for every kind of question. It is not ready and it is not open yet.

Where it has got to

Multiplication and long division have a real work block — a box for every digit you would actually write. The routing works: it climbs when you do well, drops back when you do not, and lands on the right level. The rest of the question types still need their own way of showing the work before any of it is worth trusting.

Where should this student start in maths?

Fifteen to twenty questions. It gets harder when you do well and easier when you do not, so nobody sits through a page of sums they already know or a page they cannot read. There is no time limit and no pass mark.

Get paper and a pencil first.

This is not a mental maths test. Work it out on paper exactly as you would on a worksheet, then type the answer. Taking your time is expected and costs you nothing.

No multiple choice.

Every answer is typed. With four options a student who knows nothing still scores 25%, and with no national norming data behind this test that guessing floor is the difference between a real placement and a coin toss.

What grade is the student in?

Only used to pick a starting point. Guessing low is fine, and it is not held against anyone.

Fractions like 3/4. Decimals like 1.25.
